## 1. The verse and its results

### 9.123 (Kāśī 124): Sattva Vela
Source reading (`xwfu/t.txt` lines 25759–25762; same in `xbla` 19537–19539, `yaeo`, `dli` 21098ff):

> वाग्मी शिष्टाचारधर्मस्तपस्वी नित्योत्साही निर्मलो दानशीलः ।
> तेजोविद्यारूपवान् सत्यवादी वीतारातिः सत्त्ववेलाप्रजातः ॥

IAST: *vāgmī śiṣṭācāra-dharmas tapasvī nityotsāhī nirmalo dānaśīlaḥ / tejo-vidyā-rūpavān satyavādī vītārātiḥ sattva-velā-prajātaḥ.*

Reading (mine): the one born in the Sattva Vela is eloquent, follows the conduct and dharma of the good, an ascetic, ever energetic, pure, generous, radiant, learned, handsome, truthful, free of enemies. Sastri (`sast/vol1.txt` line 35357): "eloquent, conforming to the duties and practices of the wise, devout, constantly persevering, pure, bountiful, possessed of lustre, learning and beauty, truthful and without enemies". The Gujarati (line 14366ff) has the same.

### 9.124 (Kāśī 125): Rajas and Tamas Vela
> रजोवेलाजातः सुखधनयशोरूपबलवान् जितारातिः कामातुरमतिरबन्धुप्रियमनाः ।
> तमोवेलाजातः परधनवधूको गतसुखः शठस्वामी बन्धुद्विजगुरुविरोधी चपलधीः ॥

IAST: *rajo-velā-jātaḥ sukha-dhana-yaśo-rūpa-balavān jitārātiḥ kāmātura-matir abandhu-priya-manāḥ / tamo-velā-jātaḥ para-dhana-vadhūko gata-sukhaḥ śaṭha-svāmī bandhu-dvija-guru-virodhī capala-dhīḥ.*

Reading (mine): Rajas Vela: happy, rich, famous, handsome, strong, conqueror of foes, mind love-sick, (mind) "abandhu-priya", that is not fond of kin. Tamas Vela: covets others' wealth and wife, without happiness, master of rogues, at odds with kin, brahmins and gurus, fickle-minded. (`abandhu-priya-manāḥ` can be read "fond of non-relatives" or "not fond of relatives"; Sastri: "his mind will not be kindly disposed towards his relations"; the Hindi *Vimalā* says "binā parivāra-mitra-vālā". The user's "passion and attachment to loved ones more than kin" follows the first reading; both are in the print.)
Source: `xwfu/t.txt` 25762–25765; `sast/vol1.txt` 35366ff (Sastri "Sloka 124"). Clean text in `xwfu`, `xbla`; OCR of the Sastri translation partly garbled but the sense is clear.

### 9.125 (Kāśī 126): the order
> तमः सत्त्वरजोवेलास्तमः सत्त्वं रजस्तमः ।
> भवन्त्यर्कदिनादीनामर्धयामैरनुक्रमात् ॥

IAST: *tamaḥ sattva-rajo-velās tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajas tamaḥ / bhavanty arka-dinādīnām ardha-yāmair anukramāt.*

Source: `xwfu/t.txt` 25766–25767 (clean); `xbla` 19544; `yaeo` 16825; `dli` 21104 (reads *adhayāmaiḥ* for *ardhayāmaiḥ* by OCR slip). All editions have *arka-dinādīnām* (genitive plural, "of Sunday and the others") and *ardhayāmaiḥ* (instrumental plural, "by half-yāmas").

Literal reading (mine): "Tamas, Sattva, Rajas Velas, (then) Tamas, Sattva, Rajas, Tamas: they come to be, in order, by the half-yāmas of Sunday and the other days." The seven words are seven Velas in a row: T S R T S R T.

## 2. The commentary (Sudhāśālinī, Kapileśvara Śāstrī) and the translation notes

### Sudhāśālinī on 9.126 (Kāśī numbering)
Cleanest OCR: `xbla/t.txt` lines 19546–19552; also `yaeo` 16827–16832, `dli` 21106–21112, `kashi` 22548–22557, `hindi` 22041–22046. The editions agree; where OCR differs the reading below is my normalised one.

> इदानीं वेलाफलान्युच्यन्ते । तत्र का नाम वेलेत्युच्यते । अर्कदिनादीनां सूर्यादिवासराणाम् अर्धयामैः अर्धप्रहरप्रमितैः अनुक्रमात् क्रमेण तमः सत्त्वं रजः तमः सत्त्वं रजः तमः इति वेला भवन्ति । प्रतिदिनं षोडश षोडश । रविवासरे तम आरभ्य तमः सत्त्वं रजः तमः सत्त्वं रज इत्येवं प्रकारेण षोडश । चन्द्रवासरे सत्त्वं रजः तमः सत्त्वमेवं क्रमेण षोडश । एवं प्रतिवारमुक्तक्रमेण ज्ञेयाः । प्रतिखण्डं प्रहरार्धमितमेवमष्टप्रहरेषु षोडश भवन्ति । यथा चक्रम् … जन्मकाले या वेला भवेत्तदुक्तं फलं तस्य वाच्यमिति । फलं सर्वं स्पष्टमेव ॥ १२४–१२६ ॥

IAST: *idānīṃ velā-phalāny ucyante. tatra kā nāma velety ucyate. arka-dinādīnāṃ sūryādi-vāsarāṇām ardha-yāmair ardha-prahara-pramitair anukramāt kramena tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajaḥ tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajaḥ tamaḥ iti velā bhavanti. prati-dinaṃ ṣoḍaśa ṣoḍaśa. ravi-vāsare tama ārabhya tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajaḥ tamaḥ sattvaṃ raja ity evaṃ prakāreṇa ṣoḍaśa. candra-vāsare sattvaṃ rajaḥ tamaḥ sattvam evaṃ krameṇa ṣoḍaśa. evaṃ prati-vāram ukta-krameṇa jñeyāḥ. prati-khaṇḍaṃ praharārdha-mitam evam aṣṭa-praharesu ṣoḍaśa bhavanti. yathā cakram … janma-kāle yā velā bhavet tad-uktaṃ phalaṃ tasya vācyam iti. phalaṃ sarvaṃ spaṣṭam eva.*

Reading (mine): "Now the results of the Velas. What is a Vela? Through the half-yāmas, measured by half-praharas, of Sunday and the other weekdays, in order, the Velas are Tamas, Sattva, Rajas, Tamas, Sattva, Rajas, Tamas (and so on). Sixteen each day. On Sunday, beginning with Tamas: Tamas, Sattva, Rajas, Tamas, Sattva, Rajas, in this way sixteen. On Monday, beginning with Sattva: Sattva, Rajas, Tamas, Sattva, in this order sixteen. So for each weekday in the order stated. Each segment is half a prahara; thus in eight praharas there are sixteen. As in the table … The result stated for whichever Vela there is at the time of birth is to be told for him. The results are all plain."

The table (*cakra*) follows with 7 rows (Sun to Sat) × 16 columns. OCR of the table is broken in every print (the cells of `dli` lines 21113–21122 are the least bad); see section 4.

### Sastri's translation and notes
`sast/vol1.txt` lines 35399–35420 (second copy `sast2/v.txt` 35390–35405); OCR partly garbled; my reading in brackets:

> Sloka 125. The times pervaded by the qualities of Thamas (darkness), Satva (purity) and Rajas (passion) are to be reckoned by semi-yamas (half yama = an hour and a half or 3¾ Ghatis [OCR "5)1" and ")?|"; my reading]) regularly in the order Thamas, Satva, Rajas, Thamas, Satva, Rajas, &c. from Sunday forward through the other days of the week.
> Notes. According to this sloka, each day is divided into 16 half-yamas, the first (Ardhayama) as well as the last of a Sunday is Tamasa, the first and the last of a Monday are Satvika, of a Tuesday Rajasa, of a Wednesday Thamasa, of a Thursday Satvika, of a Friday Rajasa, of a Saturday Thamasa.

(The OCR renders the adjectives as "Tamasa" for Sunday and Wednesday and Saturday, "Sataika/Satvika" for Monday and Thursday, "Rajasa" for Tuesday and Friday; where the OCR has "& tUHf't (lhaniineJa)" and "Thamafa", my reading is "Tāmasa".)

This note is Sastri's own (the Sanskrit commentary says only "sixteen each day"; the first/last statement is his). It agrees with an unbroken cycle (see section 4).

### Mishra, *Prajñāvardhinī* (2018)
`xwfu/t.txt` 25768–25776, clean:

> अत्र वेलाफलं विविच्यन्ते । सूर्यादि वासराणां अर्द्धप्रहरमितै अनुक्रमात् तम सत्वं रज: इति वेला खण्डानि भवन्ति। दिनमानस्य अष्टमांश तुल्यं तथा रात्रिमानस्य अष्टमांश तुल्यं वेलाखण्डं भवति। जन्मेष्टकाले या वेला भवेत्तदुक्तानुसारफलं वाच्यम्। रविवासरे "तमः" आरभ्य "तमः" पर्यन्तं षोडश, चन्द्र वासरे "सत्वं" आरभ्य "सत्वं" पर्यन्तं षोडश, भौम वासरे "रजः" आरभ्य "रजः" पर्यन्तं षोडश वेला खण्डानि भवन्ति। अन्यत् चक्रे स्पष्टम्।

IAST (mine): *… dina-mānasya aṣṭamāṃśa-tulyaṃ tathā rātri-mānasya aṣṭamāṃśa-tulyaṃ velā-khaṇḍaṃ bhavati. janmeṣṭa-kāle yā velā bhavet tad-uktānusāra-phalaṃ vācyam. ravi-vāsare "tamaḥ" ārabhya "tamaḥ" paryantaṃ ṣoḍaśa, candra-vāsare "sattvaṃ" ārabhya "sattvaṃ" paryantaṃ ṣoḍaśa, bhauma-vāsare "rajaḥ" ārabhya "rajaḥ" paryantaṃ ṣoḍaśa …*

Reading (mine): "A Vela-segment is equal to an eighth of the length of the day, and likewise to an eighth of the length of the night. The result of the Vela that is current at the birth-time (*janmeṣṭa-kāla*) is to be told. On Sunday sixteen, from Tamas to Tamas; on Monday sixteen from Sattva to Sattva; on Tuesday sixteen from Rajas to Rajas."

**Mishra is a modern editor, not the tradition.** His line is the only place in what was read that says the segment is proportional to the day and to the night (8 + 8 = 16). It is the natural reading of *ardha-prahara* if a prahara is a quarter of the day or night, but the text of Vaidyanātha and the Sudhāśālinī do not say so (see Q2).

## 3. The parallel teaching: the half-prahara and the eighths of the day and night

### Jātakapārijāta 2.5–2.6 itself names the *ardhaprahara* upagraha
`kashi/kashi.txt` 3681–3700; `xwfu/t.txt` 4410–4420:

> उपग्रहा भानुमुखग्रहांशाः कालादयः कष्टफलप्रदाः स्युः ॥ ५ ॥
> क्रमशः कालपरिधिधूमार्धप्रहराह्वयाः । यमकण्टककोदण्डमान्दिपातोपकेतवः ॥ ६ ॥

(*kramaśaḥ kāla-paridhi-dhūmārdhaprahara-āhvayāḥ / yamakaṇṭaka-kodaṇḍa-māndi-pātopaketavaḥ*.) Sudhāśālinī: each graha has its *aṃśa* in every day and in every night; Kāla = Sun's, Paridhi = Moon's, Dhūma = Mars's, **Ardhaprahara = Mercury's**, Yamaghaṇṭaka = Jupiter's, Kodaṇḍa = Venus's, Māndi/Gulika = Saturn's; it sends the reader to Bṛhat Parāśara Horā, Pūrvārdha, ch. 2 (in the editions read it is ch. 3). Mishra writes the same upagraha as "अर्धयाम" (line 4413).

### BPHS ch. 3 (*Grahaguṇasvarūpādhyāya*), vv. 66–69 in the Girish Chand Sharma edition
`bphs/t.txt` lines 2880–2900 (item `brihatparasarahorasastrawithenglishtranslationgirishchandsharmavolume1_547_G`; Devanagari OCR fair, English garbled):

> रविवारादिशन्यन्तं गुलिकादि निरूप्यते । दिवसानष्टधा भक्त्वा वारेशाद् गणयेत् क्रमात् ॥ ६६ ॥
> अष्टमोंऽशो निरीशः स्याच्छन्यंशो गुलिकः स्मृतः । रात्रिमप्यष्टधा कृत्वा वारेशात् पञ्चमादितः ॥ ६७ ॥
> गणयेदष्टमः खण्डो निष्पतिः परिकीर्तितः । शन्यंशो गुलिकः प्रोक्तो रव्यंशः कालसंज्ञकः ॥ ६८ ॥
> भौमांशो मृत्युरादिष्टो गुर्वंशो यमघण्टकः । सौम्यांशोऽर्धप्रहरकः स्वस्वदेशोद्भवः स्फुटः ॥ ६९ ॥

(the OCR has *diva-saniṣṭadhā*, *aṣṭapo 'ṃśo*; my reading as above.) IAST: *ravivārādi-śanyantaṃ gulikādi nirūpyate / divasān aṣṭadhā bhaktvā vāreśād gaṇayet kramāt // aṣṭamo 'ṃśo nirīśaḥ syāt śany-aṃśo gulikaḥ smṛtaḥ / rātrim apy aṣṭadhā kṛtvā vāreśāt pañcamāditaḥ // gaṇayed aṣṭamaḥ khaṇḍo niṣpatiḥ parikīrtitaḥ …* Reading (mine): the day is divided in **eight** parts and counted from the weekday's lord in order; the eighth part has no lord; the night too is divided in eight and counted from the fifth lord from the weekday's lord; Saturn's part is Gulika, the Sun's Kāla, Mars's Mṛtyu, Jupiter's Yamaghaṇṭaka, **Mercury's the Ardhaprahara**.

Phaladīpikā 25.2–4 (`phala2/t.txt` lines 16401–16530, Hindi of Hari Shankar Pathak; item `phaldipika_202310`): verse 2 *… ahar-mānavṛddhi-kṣayau tatra kāryau niśāyāṃ tu vāreśvarāt pañcamādyāḥ* ("the increase and decrease of the length of the day is to be applied there; at night counting from the fifth from the weekday lord"). The commentator (not the verse) says: the figures are for a 30-ghaṭī day and are "to be increased or decreased in the same ratio" for a longer or shorter day, and counts "from sunrise". Pathak's own comment at line 16545ff quotes Parāśara's verses above. The Phaladīpikā verse 3 lists the Ardhaprahara's ghaṭikā (14, 10, 6, 2, 26, 22, 18 for Sun to Sat), which are fixed numbers for a 30-ghaṭī day.

What this shows, as a parallel and not as proof about the Vela: in the same tradition (and in Vaidyanātha's own ch. 2) the day and the night are each cut in **eight** equal parts proportional to their length, counted from the weekday lord; the part that bears the name *ardha-prahara* is one of these eighths. The Vela segments of 9.125 (16 per vāra) fit this exactly if "prahara" is a quarter of the day or night. That inference is mine; no text read says it.

### Muhūrtacintāmaṇi 1.37–39: another set of "Ardhayāma / Kālavelā" (not the Vela of birth)
`mcH/t.txt` (item `muhurta-chintamani-of-daivagya-ramacharya-mahidhar-sharma`) lines 1253–1310; the Sanskrit commentary `mc/t.txt` (item `in.ernet.dli.2015.553968`) lines 2570–2640. The Muhūrtacintāmaṇi lists Kulika, Kaṇṭaka, **Kālavelā**, **Ardhayāma**, Yamaghaṇṭa as bad *kṣaṇas* per weekday, in sixteenths of the length of the day (Hindi comment: "the muhūrta is 2 ghaṭī but since the day varies, the sixteenth of the day is taken"), and says the Ardhayāma is always to be avoided. This is a muhūrta concept (a daytime fault) and is **not** the same as the Sattva/Rajas/Tamas Vela of birth; the name "Kālavelā" there has nothing to do with Tamas Vela. It is noted here only because it shows the same words (*vela*, *ardhayāma*) in the muhūrta tradition with proportional (day/16) lengths.

### Kālahorā in the same chapter (9.127 Kāśī / 126 Sastri): how the weekday and the night continue
`dli/dli.txt` ~21140ff, `kashi` 22600ff, `xwfu` 25779ff:
> मन्त्रिमङ्गलमार्तण्डशुक्रज्ञेन्दुशनैश्चराः । आरभ्य वारतो होरा रात्रौ पञ्चमवारतः ॥
Sudhāśālinī: "In one *ahorātra* of sixty daṇḍas there are 24 kālahorās, 12 by day and 12 by night; each segment is *sārdha-ghaṭī-dvayam* (2½ ghaṭī)", quoting Vasiṣṭha *vāra-pravṛtter gaditā dineśāt kālākhya-horā-patayaḥ krameṇa / sārdhena nāḍī-dvitayena taṣṭaḥ ṣaṣṭhaś ca ṣaṣṭhaś ca punaḥ punaḥ syāt* and Rāmācārya (Muhūrtacintāmaṇi 1.55) *vārāder ghaṭikā dvighnā svākṣa-hṛc-cheṣa-varjitāḥ sa-ekās taṣṭā nagaiḥ kāla-horeśā dina-pāt kramāt*. For the night: "on Sunday night the first is Jupiter's" (a night hora begins from the fifth from the weekday lord).
Why it matters: (a) the Vela and the Kālahorā sit in the same chapter, and the Kālahorā is the one with a stated, fixed unit (2½ ghaṭī, 24 per ahorātra, "sixty daṇḍas" = 24 hours) while the Vela has "ardha-prahara"; (b) the rule "night begins from the fifth lord" shows that the night after sunset belongs to the **same weekday** (the 13th hora is the fifth from the weekday lord counting the lord itself as first), so the weekday of the horas is sunrise to sunrise. Both are my reading of the text, not a statement about the Vela.

## 4. The answers

### Q1. Starting point of the Vela count
The Sanskrit of 9.125 and the Sudhāśālinī say only "from Sunday forward (*arkadinādīnām … anukramāt*)" and "beginning with Tamas on Sunday (*ravi-vāsare tama ārabhya*)". **They do not name sunrise, sunset or midnight.** The nearest statements: the Sudhāśālinī counts *prati-dinaṃ ṣoḍaśa* (16 per weekday) and "8 praharas = 16"; Mishra says an eighth of the day-length and an eighth of the night-length (so 8 by day, 8 by night); BPHS 3.66–67 and Phaladīpikā 25.2 (the parallel) start the day's eighths at the weekday lord and the night's at the fifth lord. By implication (mine, not stated) day segment 1 begins at sunrise and night segment 1 (the 9th of the weekday) at sunset. Certainty: not stated for the Vela; implication from parallel only.

### Q2. Fixed 90 minutes or proportional?
The text says *ardha-yāma* (half yāma) and the commentary says *ardha-prahara-pramita* (measured by half a prahara) and "8 praharas give 16". Neither the verse nor the Sudhāśālinī says how long a prahara is. Readings in the editions:
- Sastri: half-yama = "an hour and a half or 3¾ ghaṭīs" (fixed), so 16 × 90 min = 24 h. (OCR of the ghaṭī figure is garbled; the "an hour and a half" is clear; 90 min = 3¾ ghaṭī.) The Gujarati edition's tables show 3¾-ghaṭī rows (fixed).
- Mishra (2018): an eighth of the day-length and an eighth of the night-length (proportional).
- Parallels in the tradition (BPHS 3.66–67, Phaladīpikā 25.2, Jātakapārijāta 2.6 with its Ardhaprahara): day in 8, night in 8, scaled by the actual length.
**The sources disagree:** Sastri/Gujarati = fixed 90 min / 3¾ ghaṭī; Mishra = ⅛ of the actual day and of the actual night. Neither is in the verse. The classical wording "ardha-yāma/ardha-prahara" and the eight-part rule of Parāśara favour proportional, the notes of the two translations favour fixed. **Cannot be settled from the text; the program must say it is unconfirmed**, and may offer the two as a setting (the user has forbidden inventing the length of the periods).

### Q3. Which ardha-yāma of Sunday is first
The first half-yāma of Sunday is **Tamas** ("on Sunday beginning with Tamas", Sudhāśālinī; Sastri's note: "the first … of a Sunday is Tāmasa"). It is the first segment of the weekday, in the sense of Q1. Which instant "the first" is cannot be settled beyond that (Q1). Certainty: clean text (commentary), the Sastri note agrees.

### Q4. Unbroken cycle or restart each day; what the seven words are
- **Unbroken.** The Sudhāśālinī says that on Sunday the 16 begin with Tamas, on Monday "beginning with Sattva", and "so for each weekday in the order stated" (*evaṃ prativāram ukta-krameṇa jñeyāḥ*). Sastri's note lists the first and last of each day: Sun T…T, Mon S…S, Tue R…R, Wed T…T, Thu S…S, Fri R…R, Sat T…T. Mishra: Sunday T to T, Monday S to S, Tuesday R to R. This is exactly the continuous cycle T S R T S R … over 7 × 16 = 112 segments: since 16 ≡ 1 (mod 3), the first Vela of each weekday advances by one place in the cycle, so the first Velas of Sun…Sat are T S R T S R T, which are the seven words of the verse. The cycle does not restart: if it restarted each day, Monday would begin with Tamas, which the commentary denies.
- **The seven words** are the seven first-Velas of Sunday to Saturday **and** also the first seven segments of Sunday (the cycle of three repeated). The Sudhāśālinī takes them as the opening seven of the repeating series ("tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajaḥ tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajaḥ tamaḥ iti velā bhavanti"). The user's reading (a cycle of three through the weekdays) agrees with the commentary, and the "seven weekday" reading gives the same first Vela per day, so the two readings differ only in what the 16 segments of one day are; the commentary settles it: 16 per day.
- Derived table (mine, by the rule; with the Sastri first/last list it is checked on the first and last of each day, and my reading of the Gujarati night table's first row agrees): the Vela of weekday d (Sun = 0 … Sat = 6) and segment k (1 … 16, day segments 1–8, night segments 9–16) is the cycle T, S, R at the number (16·d + k) mod 3 = 1, 2, 0.

| Day | Segments 1–8 (day) | Segments 9–16 (night) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sun | T S R T S R T S | R T S R T S R T |
| Mon | S R T S R T S R | T S R T S R T S |
| Tue | R T S R T S R T | S R T S R T S R |
| Wed | T S R T S R T S | R T S R T S R T |
| Thu | S R T S R T S R | T S R T S R T S |
| Fri | R T S R T S R T | S R T S R T S R |
| Sat | T S R T S R T S | R T S R T S R T |

(T = Tamas, S = Sattva, R = Rajas.) The printed *cakra* of the Sudhāśālinī (`dli` lines 21113–21122 and the other editions) is OCR-broken, so the table above is a derivation, not a transcript; the first column agrees with the "first of each day" list, and the parts of the OCR that can be read agree. A view of the printed page was not obtained (the archive.org page-image route returned the title page).

### Q5. Change of the weekday: sunrise or midnight
Not stated in the Vela verses or commentary. Indirect: the Kālahorā of the same chapter runs 12 by day and 12 by night from the weekday lord, and the night begins from the fifth lord, which makes the night belong to the weekday of the preceding day (the Vedic/sunrise vāra); Parāśara and the Phaladīpikā reckon the day's eighths from the weekday lord. This points to the vāra from sunrise to sunrise, but **no text read says so for the Vela.** Certainty: low (inference).

### Q6. How the birth moment is placed
The only rule is: "the Vela that exists at the birth-time, its result is to be told" (Sudhāśālinī: *janma-kāle yā velā bhavet tad-uktaṃ phalaṃ tasya vācyam*; Mishra: *janmeṣṭa-kāle*). The method is by the table: the weekday of birth and the position of the birth moment among the 16 segments. For a day birth, the ghaṭī since sunrise divided by the segment length; for a night birth, since sunset (my reading of the Gujarati's separate day and night tables, line 14400ff, not a statement). No worked example for the Vela was found in the Sudhāśālinī, Sastri, the Gujarati or Mishra; the worked examples of Mishra's ch. 2 are for the upagrahas only (`xwfu` 4540–4565: Gulika, Yamaghaṇṭa, Ardhayāma, Kāla for a Monday of 31;37 ghaṭī day, scaled in proportion to the day-length, from the 30-ghaṭī figures).

## 6. Where the sources disagree, and what is not settled

- Verse numbers: Sastri and the Gujarati have 123/124/125, the Kāśī-Chowkhamba editions 124/125/126 (the one-number shift is before the triṃśāṃśa verse).
- Fixed 3¾ ghaṭī (Sastri, Gujarati tables) against ⅛ of the day and night (Mishra, 2018). This is the one choice the program cannot take from the text. Do not state either as classical fact; state that the Sanskrit only says half-prahara.
- The starting instant (sunrise, sunset) and the changing of the weekday are not given anywhere for the Vela. State it as unconfirmed and (if the user wants a default) mark it "by analogy with Parāśara's upagraha rule, not the text".
- The Sunday-first reading of the user matches the commentary ("tamas, sattva, rajas through the half-yāmas of the weekdays from Sunday"), with the difference that the commentary makes it **16 half-yāmas per weekday** (day and night together), and that the verse's seven words are the pattern, not the seven weekdays.
- Not found: any commentary older than the Sudhāśālinī; any other classical statement of the Sattva/Rajas/Tamas Vela of birth (not found in the BPHS, Phaladīpikā or Muhūrtacintāmaṇi pages read; Sārāvalī, Jātakabharaṇa, Horāratna and the Telugu/Tamil prints were not searched); a worked example of the Vela.
